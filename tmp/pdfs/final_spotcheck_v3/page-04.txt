Figure 2:ICL examples have a meaningful
but limited effect on controllability.
ICL experiments.We evaluate two types of
in-context learning (ICL) examples: ‘user-ICL’
where we place the compliant example in the user
prompt and ‘asst-ICL’ where we place a complete
user + assistant response turn in context (where
the compliant trace is prepended to the assistant
response). We run these experiments on Qwen-3-
14b, Qwen-3-32b, Olmo-3.1-32b, and GLM-5.2
and evaluate controllability on our main 8 control
tasks.
Figure 2 shows that ICL examples have a mean-
ingful but limited effect on increasing controlla-
bility. We see that GLM-5.2 is already quite con-
trollable, and under ‘asst-ICL’, base control com-
pliance rises from 50.0% to 64.4%. Qwen3-(14 and 32b) and Olmo-3.1-32b remain relatively un-
controllable even with ICL examples (though Qwen-3-32b sees a sizable increase with user-ICL).
Figure 9 shows per-control-type performance. Suppression tasks generally benefit from ICL, al-
though gains depend on the model and ICL type. Addition control types see more inconsistent
benefits: ‘User-ICL’ significantly increases ‘Sentence endings‘ compliance for both Qwen3-(14b
and 32b) and moderately for GLM-5.2. All style control types remain close to 0 for Qwen3-(14b
and 32b) and Olmo3.1-32b, and see meaningful increases for GLM-5.2. Interestingly, we find that
for all prompt strategies, over 63% of responses with scores below 0.5 fail at the first token.
