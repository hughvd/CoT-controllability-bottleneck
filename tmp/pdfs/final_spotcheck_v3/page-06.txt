Models can maintain control over their CoT.A simple exploit is to then prefill reasoning traces
with sentences that are compliant with the control instruction and see if the model can maintain

We evaluate controllability on our main 8 control tasks using 30 questions per control task and five

Indeed, in Figure 5 we see that this works. Seeding with just one token has a substantial impact
on CoT-controllability, with more tokens corresponding to a marginal increase in controllability.
Additionally, we see that the presence of instructions improves controllability, which supports that
models CoT are in fact affected by these control instructions (but often do not reflect that in the
decoded tokens). Figure 10 in Appendix B shows per control type performance. Prefilling substan-
tially improves many addition and style tasks, particularly for Qwen and OLMo. Word omission
changes comparatively little, while first-person avoidance benefits more from longer prefixes. This
is opposite to what we found for ICL, where suppression control types were the most affected.

Figure 6 shows that first-token banning increases average compliance from 0.078 to 0.254 for
Qwen3-32B and from 0.029 to 0.225 for OLMo-3.1-32B. Combining banning with user-ICL fur-
ther increases compliance to 0.490 and 0.374, respectively, exceeding either intervention alone.
These gains suggest that demonstrations can become more effective when models are prevented
from choosing their dominant openings. The combination is particularly effective for Qwen up-
percase and leetspeak, which reach 0.775 and 0.652 despite remaining near zero with user-ICL
alone, and for OLMo numbering and leetspeak, which reach 0.418 and 0.747 (Appendix Figure 12).
However, the interventions do not combine favorably on every task: Qwen sentence-ending compli-
ance is higher with user-ICL alone, while OLMo Spanish compliance is higher with banning alone.
Unlike compliant prefilling, banning leaves the alternative opening to the model. Together, these
results support an initiation bottleneck that limits the effectiveness of prompting, while showing that
escaping dominant openings does not uniformly recover control.
